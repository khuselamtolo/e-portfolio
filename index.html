Who I am

Engineer first, energy-systems thinker always.

I'm a final-year Electrical and Electronic Engineering student at UCT, specialising in power systems. What draws me to this field isn't just the mathematics of a swing equation or the elegance of a well-tuned control loop — it's the fact that the decisions engineers make about grids, dispatch, and access shape people's daily lives, often invisibly.

That's part of why I've spent as much time reading about energy as political economy — Eskom's tariff structures, NERSA's regulatory role, the mechanics of the South African Wholesale Electricity Market — as I have in the lab. I want to understand not just how a system works, but why it was built that way, and who it serves.

My current focus is predictive, AI-assisted control for microgrids: using machine learning to forecast load and solar generation, then using that forecast to make smarter dispatch decisions than a conventional rule-based controller can. It sits at the exact point where my interests in control theory, data-driven methods, and South African energy policy meet.

Outside of engineering, I produce music in my spare time, and I'm currently developing a side concept for a solar-powered, modular vertical farming rig aimed at suburban South African homes — proof, to me, that good systems thinking travels well beyond the substation.

Flagship project

Predictive Energy Management Using AI in Microgrids

My 40-credit capstone research project, supervised by A/Prof. David Oyedokun in UCT's Department of Electrical Engineering.

40 credits Supervisor: A/Prof. D. Oyedokun In progress
  • Forecasting. Machine-learning models predict short-horizon load demand and solar PV generation, feeding real-world uncertainty into the control problem instead of assuming a fixed schedule.
  • Predictive dispatch. An MPC-style controller uses those forecasts to decide, ahead of time, how to balance grid import, battery storage, and PV output.
  • Benchmarking. Performance is tested against a conventional rule-based baseline controller, simulated in DigSILENT PowerFactory, to quantify what predictive control actually buys you.
  • Grounded in policy. The project is framed against real South African constraints — NERSA's regulatory posture, Eskom's Homeflex tariff structure, and the small-scale embedded generation (SSEG) landscape.
